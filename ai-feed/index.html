EU AI Act Enforcement Goes Live: Every Chatbot Must Declare AI, Every Deepfake Must Be Labeled

As of August 2, the European Commission's AI Office began enforcing Article 50 of the EU AI Act, requiring chatbots and interactive AI systems to tell users they are dealing with AI, and requiring AI-generated or AI-altered content to carry machine-readable marks. Non-compliance carries fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. The December 2 grace date for models already on the market is now the operational deadline every AI provider is racing toward.

What's moving
  • Enforcement is immediate for systems placed on the market after August 2 - no grace period applies to new releases, meaning every product shipped since then is under live legal exposure.
  • National market surveillance authorities and the EU AI Office can inspect models, restrict market access, and issue fines; the first enforcement actions are expected in the coming months.
  • China activated its own AI agent regulations on July 15 - the first national framework to treat AI agents as a distinct legal category - meaning every major deployment now sits inside some regulatory envelope.
