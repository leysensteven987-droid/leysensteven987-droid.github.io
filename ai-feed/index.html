Nvidia's AVO Agent Clears All 183 ARC-AGI-3 Levels - Wrapped Around Claude Opus 5, Which Scores 30% Alone

On August 22 Nvidia disclosed that AVO, its general-purpose coding agent, completed all 183 levels across all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 environments and posted a perfect 100.00 on the benchmark's Relative Human Action Efficiency metric - working out what to do with no instructions, no stated rules and no stated goals. The striking part is the substrate: AVO runs on Claude Opus 5, which scores 30.2% on the same benchmark when tested bare. The harness - memory, tool use, recovery from failure, sustained context across a long task - accounted for the remaining seventy points. Nvidia published results on the public set only, not the private set ARC has historically used to guard against overfitting.

What's moving
  • AVO cleared all 183 levels in 6,624 actions, roughly 12% fewer than VISTA, the previous best comparable system - efficiency, not just completion, is now the scored axis.
  • The 30.2%-to-100% gap on an unchanged model is the clearest published evidence yet that agent scaffolding, not raw model capability, is the binding constraint on long-horizon autonomy.
  • Public-set-only disclosure leaves the result incomplete as a general-reasoning claim; ARC's private set is the test that would settle it.

Anthropic Hires Google's TPU Founder as Broadcom Seeks up to $100B in Debt to Build Its Silicon

Bloomberg reported on August 21 that Anthropic has hired Amir Salek - who helped lead Google's TPU programme and delivered its first seven generations of chips - into its compute team, reporting to James Bradbury. The day before, Broadcom was reported in talks with lenders to raise more than $60 billion, and potentially as much as $100 billion across senior-secured and junior tranches, to finance custom AI chip capacity for customers including Anthropic. Read together, the two signals describe one move: Anthropic standing up an in-house silicon function rather than remaining a pure buyer of Nvidia and Google compute.

What's moving
  • Salek's arrival gives Anthropic an executive who has designed and scaled seven TPU generations, and the company has begun hiring for its own silicon operation.
  • The Broadcom raise builds on the June Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone partnership financing a $35 billion expansion of Anthropic's compute - an initial one gigawatt against a stated target of more than 20 gigawatts for leading AI labs by 2028.
  • Debt, not equity, is now the instrument funding frontier compute, which shifts the downside from AI investors onto credit markets.

OpenAI Pauses Frontier RL Training After Astra Approaches the Critical Cyber Threshold

On August 18 OpenAI published that preliminary evidence indicates Astra, one of its upcoming models, may meet the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. The company slowed scaling in response: a two-week pause on reinforcement-learning training for models intended for deployment, with its largest planned frontier RL run still on hold. Once Astra was determined on August 7 to have critical cyber capabilities, OpenAI added a monitoring requirement covering all Astra inference that uses tools.

What's moving
  • OpenAI put monitoring overhead at roughly 20% of the inference compute being monitored - the first public number on what a frontier safety case actually costs in compute.
  • This is the first time a major lab has publicly halted a frontier training run on capability-threshold grounds rather than in response to an incident with a released model.
  • Anthropic moved the opposite way in the same week, pushing frontier cyber capability out to defenders - two labs, one dual-use problem, visibly diverging answers.

Nvidia Pays $7B for Poolside Without Buying It - the License-Plus-Hire Playbook Goes Mainstream

On August 21 Nvidia was reported to have committed roughly $7 billion to Poolside: $6 billion for a non-exclusive licence to the Model Factory, the system Poolside uses to build its open-weight Laguna coding models, plus $1 billion of equity at a $12 billion pre-money valuation, with job offers extended to 109 Poolside employees. Poolside's leadership told investors flatly that this is not an acquisition and not an acquihire, and the three founders are staying. It is the same structure Nvidia used with Groq.

What's moving

  • Paying $6 billion for the pipeline that produces models, rather than for the models themselves, prices the model factory above the model.
  • NVDA closed the week roughly 5% lower on the news, ahead of Q2 FY27 earnings on August 26.

Pennsylvania Signs the Strictest AI Data Centre Rules in the US - and Gives Towns a Veto

On August 18 Governor Josh Shapiro signed an executive order that stops state environmental permitting for a data centre unless the developer has already secured local approval and made a legally binding commitment to his GRID requirements: paying the full electricity cost of the facility and sourcing a significant share of that power from clean energy. All AI data centre projects were removed from Pennsylvania's Permit Fast Track Program immediately and will not be considered for it in future. Non-disclosure agreements on data centre projects are no longer permissible, and locally approved projects must negotiate a community benefits agreement.

What's moving
  • A governor who had actively courted data centre investment reversed into the tightest guardrails in the country - the clearest sign yet that pass-through of grid cost has become the politically decisive issue.
  • Making local approval a precondition for state permitting inverts the usual order and hands municipalities a practical veto over gigawatt-scale siting.
  • The ban on non-disclosure agreements forces the economics of these deals into public view, which is exactly where the political heat has been coming from.

ChatGPT Ads Land in 31 European Markets - Weeks After the AI Act Switched On

On August 19 OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Ads to 31 European markets, rolling out through OpenAI Ads Solutions and agency partners. It is the fastest geographic expansion of the ads product since launch, and it puts a monetised surface in front of European users in the same month the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties came into force - the rules requiring AI systems to disclose that they are AI and requiring AI-generated content to carry machine-readable marks.

What's moving
  • Advertising inside an assistant is a different unit of inventory to search or feed: the placement sits inside an answer, which is exactly where disclosure rules bite hardest.
  • Thirty-one markets at once implies legal review cleared broadly rather than country by country - a signal about how OpenAI reads the AI Act's transparency perimeter.
  • Non-compliance with Article 50 runs to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, with a 2 December 2026 grace date for generative systems already on the market.
