Anthropic Ships Claude Opus 5 at Half the Price of Fable - Effort Toggle and ARC-AGI-3 Record Rewrite What Frontier Means

On July 24, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5, a model that matches or beats Claude Fable 5 on most benchmarks at exactly half the input token price and introduces a per-request effort toggle - low, medium, or high - letting developers trade reasoning depth for cost within a single model. What's moving

The EU AI Act's Biggest Enforcement Date Arrives in One Week - August 2 Puts Every High-Risk AI Deployer in Europe on the Clock

August 2, 2026 is one week away and marks the moment Articles 9 through 17 and Article 26 of the EU AI Act become enforceable for deployers of high-risk AI systems across the European market. Article 26 requires human oversight assignment, six-month log retention, incident reporting to market surveillance authorities, and mandatory notification to workers where AI is used in employment decisions. The obligations apply to any company - European or not - that operates Annex III high-risk systems including HR screening tools, credit scoring, critical infrastructure, and biometric categorization.

What's moving
  • The EU Parliament approved a provisional Digital Omnibus deal on June 16 that would push the Annex III deployer deadline to December 2, 2027, but the agreement still needs formal Council adoption and Official Journal publication - until those formalities land, August 2 remains the operative date enforcement bodies can act on
  • Fines reach the higher of 15 million euros or 3% of global annual turnover, meaning a major US AI platform operating high-risk systems in the EU without completing the Articles 9-17 conformity framework faces nine-figure exposure if enforcement begins on schedule
  • The Commission published implementation guidelines on July 20 and its Cybersecurity-AI Action Plan on July 7, compressing the window between authoritative guidance and enforcement start to under two weeks - leaving compliance teams scrambling to close documentation, logging, and oversight gaps before the clock runs out

EU Orders Google to Give Rival AI Assistants Gemini's Eleven Android Privileges - Non-Compliance Fines Could Top $35 Billion

On July 16, 2026, the European Commission issued two binding Digital Markets Act decisions requiring Google to open eleven Android system integration points - voice activation, background task execution, screen context reading, on-device model access, and messaging reply suggestions among them - to competing AI assistants that currently have no access to those hooks. A parallel decision requires Google to share its search ranking and click data with rival AI services at the same frequency Google uses it internally, beginning January 2027. Non-compliance fines can reach 10% of Alphabet's global annual turnover, currently above $35 billion.

What's moving
  • Google must equalise access to all eleven Android features by August 1, 2027, with one additional provision - concurrent wake-word support allowing two assistants to listen simultaneously - required a year later by August 2028; the changes affect every Android device sold in Europe and could be adopted globally if Google opts for a single implementation
  • The search data-sharing mandate forces Google to give competing AI assistants real-time access to the same ranking signals and behavioral click data that power Google's own results, transforming how third-party AI can ground answers in current web information
  • Google's Android president Sameer Samat publicly stated the mandates risk user privacy and security; the company has no right to delay implementation pending appeal, and the Commission can assess interim compliance measures - making this the highest-stakes live AI competition intervention any regulator has undertaken

Kimi K3's 1.4-Terabyte Weights Land July 27 - The Largest Open Model Ever Ships With a 51% Hallucination Rate Moonshot Did Not Chart

In two days, on July 27, Moonshot AI releases the open weights of Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts model and the largest open AI release in history. While the launch competes directly with Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on coding and reasoning benchmarks, independent evaluators have found a 51% hallucination rate on standard factual tasks that does not appear in Moonshot's official benchmark comparisons. The MXFP4-quantized weights total 1.4 terabytes - down from a 5.6 TB FP16 equivalent - and still require 64 or more accelerators in supernode configurations to run.

What's moving
  • Only 16 of 896 expert modules activate per token - roughly 50 billion active parameters per forward pass - and Kimi Delta Attention delivers up to 6.3 times faster decoding at long contexts; the model ships under a Modified MIT license permitting commercial fine-tuning, redistribution, and on-premise deployment without API constraints
  • The 51% hallucination rate finding is likely to dominate community analysis once weights are downloadable: researchers will independently verify both the omission from Moonshot's charts and whether targeted fine-tuning on factual datasets can close the gap, setting up K3 as the most scrutinized open-weight release in years
  • For infrastructure teams planning to self-host, Moonshot's own guidance calls for 64-plus accelerators in supernode configurations even at MXFP4 precision - a threshold that rules out single-node deployment on any current server generation and makes K3 a data-center-only proposition at launch
