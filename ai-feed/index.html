EU Opens Bidding for 7 AI Gigafactories Backed by EUR 10B in Public Funds

The European Commission launched its formal call for AI Gigafactory proposals on July 30, 2026, committing EUR 10 billion ($11.4B) in public financing and targeting EUR 30 billion total when private investment is added. Seven planned sites will each host at least 100,000 cutting-edge AI chips, together more than doubling the EU's total AI computing power. The bid window closes November 12, 2026, and awards are expected by mid-2027.

What's moving
  • Each gigafactory must be roughly four times more powerful than any currently operating EU AI data center, filling a critical gap left by the bloc's fragmented 19-center network that was built for older, smaller workloads
  • Commission executive vice president Henna Virkkunen called gigafactory-scale compute "a strategic necessity," framing the initiative as a sovereignty play to reduce Europe's dependence on US and Chinese cloud providers who could "weaponize" that access
  • The program stacks on top of the EUR 200B InvestAI initiative and signals Europe's shift from AI regulation alone - the focus of the EU AI Act - to direct competition for compute infrastructure, the upstream chokepoint regulators had previously left to markets

MCP Goes Stateless - The July 28 Spec Overhaul Unlocks Serverless AI Agent Deployments

The Model Context Protocol's 2026-07-28 specification, finalized on July 28, 2026, is the most significant rewrite of the protocol since its launch - converting MCP from a stateful bidirectional channel into a standard request/response model. The change lets MCP servers run on serverless and edge infrastructure without maintaining session state, removing the biggest deployment bottleneck for production AI agent integrations. The protocol now handles 400 million monthly SDK downloads, four times higher than at the start of 2026.

What's moving
  • Dropping the required session handshake means MCP servers can cold-start per request, scale to zero between calls, and run inside standard serverless platforms - the old stateful design forced dedicated long-lived processes that made autoscaling for enterprise workloads impractical
  • Authorization is now hardened to align with production OAuth 2.0 and OIDC, letting MCP connectors plug directly into enterprise identity systems like Entra or Okta without the custom middleware bridges that had been a recurring deployment pain point
  • A versioned extensions framework standardizes how developers add MCP Apps (interactive UIs) and Tasks (long-running work) without touching the core protocol spec, giving the growing tooling ecosystem a stable surface as the protocol evolves

Moonshot AI Drops Kimi K3 - the World's Largest Open Model at 2.8 Trillion Parameters

China's Moonshot AI, backed by Alibaba, released Kimi K3 on July 16, 2026, and published full weights on July 27, making it the largest open-weight model in the world at 2.8 trillion parameters. AI Scans 2.6 Million Cancer Studies and Flags 250,000 with Paper-Mill Fingerprints

A BERT-based classifier built at Queensland University of Technology analyzed every English-language cancer research paper published from 1999 to 2024 - 2.6 million total - and flagged more than 250,000 as sharing linguistic patterns with papers already confirmed to be from fraudulent "paper mills." Published in The BMJ in July 2026, the study is the first systematic AI audit of a major scientific field at population scale, and it arrives precisely as generative AI is accelerating the production of fake research on the other side.

What's moving
  • The classifier scores papers using only their title and abstract and runs at 91% accuracy against historically confirmed paper-mill examples - but researchers flag an 82.8% miss rate on newer AI-generated fakes, meaning the real scope of fraudulent cancer literature could be substantially larger than 250,000 papers
  • More than 170,000 flagged papers carry China-affiliated institutions, roughly 36% of China's cancer research output over the 25-year window - a finding that has triggered immediate calls for retraction reviews at major publishers including Elsevier and Springer
  • The tool and full dataset are being released openly so other scientific fields can run comparable sweeps, raising the prospect of a permanent AI integrity layer across global scientific publishing at a moment when generative AI is dissolving the very linguistic fingerprints the detector depends on
