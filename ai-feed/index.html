What's moving

What's moving
  • The 15 million euro emotion recognition fine is the most legally significant of the three because it invokes a prohibited-practice category under Article 5 rather than a documentation compliance failure - it shows the EU will enforce bans, not just paperwork rules.
  • All three enforcement targets are core commercial AI use cases - hiring, credit, and retail surveillance - the exact categories enterprise AI buyers have been deploying most aggressively over the past two years.
  • The maximum fine for a prohibited AI practice is 35 million euros or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher; these first fines are well below the ceiling, leaving substantial room for escalation against non-compliant systems.

What's moving

  • A 19% gap in a single age cohort positions these workers as a leading indicator: older workers in the same occupations will face analogous pressure as AI capability spreads upward through career-stage exposure over the next few years.
  • Economy-wide stability coexisting with concentrated disruption matches the historical pattern of major technological transitions - the finding makes it harder to defer the policy and education response as anecdotal or speculative.
